## 5) Voice and tone standards

### Voice principles (always true)
- Clear over clever
- Credible over promotional
- Specific over vague
- Confident over aggressive
- Human over robotic

### Tone by context
- Product pages: confident, concise, practical
- Security content: precise, evidence-based, low-hype
- Executive content: strategic and outcome-driven
- Social content: energetic but still professional
- Support and lifecycle emails: reassuring and direct

### Readability targets
- Prefer short-to-medium sentences
- Explain one key idea per paragraph
- Use concrete nouns and active verbs
- Avoid jargon stacking
- Avoid exaggerated adjectives

## 6) Writing style guide

### Editorial style
- Use US English unless channel requires otherwise
- Use serial comma (Oxford comma)
- Use sentence case for body copy and most headings
- Use title case for formal section headers in docs/slides
- Keep punctuation clean and minimal

### Grammar and formatting
- Use "AI-Harness" with hyphen and capitalization exactly as shown
- Prefer "human + AI" in high-level messaging
- Prefer "AI agents" in explanatory copy
- Use en dashes for ranges where appropriate (e.g., 1-10 may remain as UI input labels if needed)
- Use numerals for metrics and percentages

### Inclusive language
- Use neutral and inclusive phrasing
- Avoid language that implies replacement of people with AI
- Prefer "augment", "assist", "support", "partner with"

## 8) Claims and evidence policy (critical)

All external-facing claims must be supportable.

### Allowed claim types
- Product capability claims (features that exist in product/roadmap context)
- Architectural claims (deployment flexibility, integrations, controls)
- Process claims (human-in-the-loop, approval gates, audit logging)
- Outcome claims when qualified (benchmarks, customer-reported, pilot-specific)

### Required qualifiers for performance claims
If using numbers (e.g., throughput, cycle time, cost):
- Add context ("customer-reported", "pilot benchmark", "based on typical deployment patterns")
- Avoid universal language ("always", "guaranteed", "for all customers")
- Avoid implying legal or compliance certification beyond what is stated

### Compliance-sensitive language rules
- Do not state certifications not achieved
- Do not imply legal advice
- Do not make regulatory guarantees
- Use "aligned with", "designed for", or "supports" unless formally certified and approved wording is available

## 9) Messaging do's and don'ts

### Must haves (non-negotiable)
- Center human accountability and governance in AI narratives
- Tie features to business outcomes
- Explain AI-Harness as workflow-embedded, not chat-only
- Maintain enterprise credibility (precision, clarity, substantiation)
- Include an appropriate CTA for the funnel stage
- Keep tone respectful, confident, and practical

### Good to haves (recommended)
- Include role-specific value framing
- Include industry-relevant examples when possible
- Use "before/after" operational scenarios
- Add lightweight proof structure (problem -> approach -> outcome)
- Use consistent visual language in design assets (clean, modern, high-trust)

### Things to avoid
- Overpromising outcomes
- Fear-based messaging
- Unqualified superlatives ("best", "only", "perfect")
- Overuse of AI buzzwords without operational meaning
- Contradictory claims across channels
- Long generic paragraphs with no buyer relevance

- Keep one insight per post

- Be conservative and exact
- Use factual and verifiable wording
- Include controls, ownership, and process details
- Avoid broad compliance claims without legal review

## 20) Claim risk matrix and approval levels

Use this matrix before publishing external content.

### Low risk claims (self-serve publish)
- Describing existing product capabilities
- Describing workflow patterns and governance approaches
- Non-quantified value language ("improves visibility", "supports accountability")

### Medium risk claims (marketing + product review)
- Comparative language ("faster", "more consistent") without hard numbers
- Integration breadth claims
- Industry suitability claims

### High risk claims (marketing + legal/security review required)
- Quantified outcomes
- Compliance/certification references
- Data handling, security architecture specifics
- Competitive superiority claims ("best", "only", "first")

### Prohibited without executive/legal sign-off
- Regulatory guarantees
- Absolute security guarantees
- Unverified certifications or audits
- Any fabricated customer evidence

## 21) Approved vs disallowed language examples

### Approved patterns
- "Designed for governed AI execution in enterprise workflows."
- "Customer-reported outcomes indicate faster cycle times in repeatable operations."
- "Supports policy-based approvals and auditable action history."
- "Built to help teams scale AI adoption with control and accountability."

### Disallowed patterns
- "Guaranteed compliance in every jurisdiction."
- "Zero-risk AI operations."
- "Instant ROI for every team."
- "Replaces operations teams entirely."

### Rewrite guide
- Weak: "Our AI is the best."
- Better: "AI-Harness gives enterprise teams a governed way to deploy and manage AI agents in core workflows."

- Weak: "You will get 4x productivity."
- Better: "Some deployments report multi-fold throughput gains; results vary by process maturity and adoption model."

- Must include at least one governance/control reference
- Must avoid replacement-of-human framing
- Must avoid excessive superlatives

### Claims lint
- Any number-based claim has qualifier context
- No unverified certification claims
- No legal/compliance guarantees
